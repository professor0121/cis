The secret to thinking quickly in a Group Discussion (GD) or a situational task is not to know everything. It's to have a framework that helps you organize your thoughts within 15–30 seconds.

Since you're preparing for a fresher Software Developer role, you may face:
- General GD topics: AI, social media, education, technology.
- Technical topics: AI vs human intelligence, Java vs Python, cloud computing.
- Situational tasks: Team conflicts, project deadlines, production bugs, handling mistakes.
- Workplace scenarios: Disagreements, leadership, decision-making.
Let's build a practical technique.
1. Use the PREP technique for any GD topic
PREP Framework
1. P — Point
   
   State your opinion clearly.
2. R — Reason
   
   Explain why you think so.
3. E — Example
   
   Give a real-world example.
4. P — Point
   
   Conclude your argument.

Example topic: Is AI beneficial or harmful?
- Point: I believe AI is beneficial when used responsibly.
- Reason: It improves productivity and reduces repetitive work.
- Example: Developers use AI tools to debug code and generate test cases.
- Point: Therefore, AI should be treated as a tool that supports human intelligence, not replaces critical thinking.

You can use this framework for almost any GD topic.
2. Use the 5-step framework for situational tasks
For situational questions, think like a responsible software engineer.
5-step decision framework
1. Understand the problem
   
   What exactly happened?
2. Identify priorities
   
   What is most important: deadline, quality, teamwork, customer satisfaction, or security?
3. Think of possible solutions
   
   Consider at least two possible approaches.
4. Take responsibility and communicate
   
   Explain whom you would inform and what action you would take.
5. Give a positive outcome
   
   Explain how you would prevent the problem from happening again.

Example situational question
Situation: Your project deadline is tomorrow, but one of your teammates has not completed their assigned task. What will you do?

Don't panic. Think through these five steps:
1. Understand why the task is incomplete.
2. Identify which parts are essential for delivery.
3. Offer help and divide the remaining work.
4. Communicate the revised plan to the team lead.
5. Complete the critical tasks and review the final work.
Your answer could be:

"I would first communicate with my teammate to understand the reason for the delay. Instead of blaming anyone, I would identify the pending tasks and prioritize the critical ones. I would offer my support, redistribute the workload if necessary, and inform the team lead about our progress. My main focus would be to deliver quality work within the deadline while maintaining teamwork."

This answer demonstrates teamwork, ownership, communication, and problem-solving.

Keep these five idea categories in your mind
Almost every general GD topic can be discussed through these perspectives:
Perspective	Questions to ask yourself
Education	How does it affect students?
Economy	How does it affect jobs and business?
Technology	How does technology influence it?
Society	How does it affect people?
Future	What might happen next?

Example: Topic — Work from Home vs Work from Office
- Education/learning: Opportunities to learn from colleagues.
- Economy: Travel and office expenses.
- Technology: Remote collaboration tools.
- Society: Work-life balance and social interaction.
- Future: Hybrid working models.
Now you have five possible directions without needing expert knowledge.
4. Five sentences that will help you in GD
Learn these sentence structures rather than memorizing complete answers.
Situation	What to say
Starting	"In my opinion, this topic has several important aspects."
Giving an opinion	"I believe this is important because..."
Adding a point	"I would like to add another perspective..."
Disagreeing	"I respect your opinion, but I see this differently..."
Concluding	"To summarize, I believe a balanced approach would be best."

Important: Don't wait for the perfect answer. A simple, logical point delivered confidently is better than remaining silent.

6. My recommended daily routine
Spend just 15 minutes every day:
- 5 minutes: Pick a random GD topic and generate three points.
- 5 minutes: Solve one situational question aloud.
- 5 minutes: Record your voice and identify hesitation, grammar mistakes, and unclear explanations.
Your target should be to think within 30 seconds and deliver a structured answer within 60–90 seconds.
